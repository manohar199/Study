1. **Values of \\(1\text{ Fermi}\\) and \\(1\text{ Angstrom}\\) in metres:**
   * **\\(1\text{ Fermi (femtometre, fm)}\\):** \\(1\text{ fm} = 10^{-15}\text{ m}\\). Standard SI sub-multiple prefix: **femto** (\\(10^{-15}\\)).
   * **\\(1\text{ Angstrom (\AA)}\\):** \\(1\text{ \AA} = 10^{-10}\text{ m}\\). Non-SI metric unit of length commonly used in atomic physics (\\(10^{-10}\text{ m} = 0.1\text{ nm}\\)).

2. **Typographical & SI Rule Errors in the Sentence:**
   * *Original Sentence:* *"An electric motor of power 5.5 H.P. consumes 4.10 KWs. of energy in 20 Secs."*
   * *Errors Identified:*
     1. **`H.P.`**: Non-SI unit symbol with periods. In SI, power must be expressed in watts (\\(\text{W}\\)) or kilowatts (\\(\text{kW}\\)) (\\(1\text{ hp} \approx 746\text{ W}\\), so \\(5.5\text{ hp} \approx 4.1\text{ kW}\\)).
     2. **`KWs.`**: Multiple violations—capital `K` is incorrect for the prefix kilo (must be lowercase `k`); adding `s` for pluralization is forbidden; trailing period `.` is forbidden; multiplying power by time gives energy (\\(\text{kW}\cdot\text{s}\\) or \\(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h}\\), not \\(\text{KWs.}\\)).
     3. **`Secs.`**: The SI symbol for second is strictly lowercase **`s`**. Full word abbreviation `Secs.` with capitalization, pluralization, and punctuation is illegal.
   * *Corrected SI Sentence:* *"An electric motor of power \\(4.1\text{ kW}\\) consumes \\(4.10\text{ kWh}\\) (or \\(14.76\text{ MJ}\\)) of energy in \\(20\text{ s}\\)."*

3. **Coherence of MKS vs. Non-Coherence of Gravitational MKS:**
   * **MKS System:** Coherent because the derived unit of force is formed directly by multiplying base units without non-unity multipliers: \\(1\text{ Newton (N)} = 1\text{ kg} \cdot 1\text{ m/s}^2\\) [28–29].
   * **Gravitational MKS System:** Non-coherent because the unit of force (\\(\text{kgf}\\) or \\(\text{kg-wt}\\)) introduces the non-unity numerical factor of local gravitational acceleration \\(g\\): \\$$(1\text{ kgf} = 9.80665\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2 = 9.80665\text{ N}\\)$$

### SUBTOPIC 1.4: DIMENSIONS AND DIMENSIONAL FORMULAE, DIMENSIONAL CONSTANTS AND DIMENSIONLESS QUANTITIES

---

#### 1. Concept
All derived physical quantities in nature can be constructed algebraically by multiplying or dividing base quantities raised to specific powers [19–21]. When a physical quantity is expressed in terms of the fundamental quantities (\\(M, L, T, I, K, \text{mol}, \text{cd}\\)), the exponents to which these base quantities are raised are defined as the **dimensions** of that physical quantity [19–21]. 

Dimensions specify the structural nature of a physical quantity regardless of its magnitude or the unit system used to measure it [20–21].

---

#### 2. Definition
* **Dimensions:** The powers or exponents to which fundamental (base) quantities must be raised to express a given physical quantity [19–20].
* **Dimensional Formula:** The algebraic expression that represents how and which base quantities enter into the unit of a physical quantity, enclosed in square brackets \\([ \dots ]\\) [21–22].
* **Dimensional Equation:** An equation obtained by equating a physical quantity with its dimensional formula (e.g., \\([F] = [M^1 L^1 T^{-2}]\\)) [21–22].
* **Dimensional Constants:** Physical quantities that possess fixed numerical values and non-zero physical dimensions (e.g., Universal Gravitational Constant \\(G\\), Planck's constant \\(h\\), Speed of light \\(c\\)) [35, 42–43, 53].
* **Dimensionless Quantities:** Physical quantities that have zero dimensions in all fundamental quantities (\\([M^0 L^0 T^0]\\)). They may or may not possess units [27, 49, 53–54].

---

#### 3. Physical Meaning
The dimensional formula isolates the essential physical nature of a quantity [20–21]. For example, whether speed is expressed in \\(\text{m/s}\\), \\(\text{km/h}\\), or \\(\text{cm/s}\\), its dimensional nature remains fundamentally \\([L T^{-1}]\\) (Length per unit Time) [20–21]. 

Dimensions ignore pure numerical constants, directions, and vector properties, focusing purely on how the magnitude scales with base physical quantities [20, 22–23].

---

#### 4. Law / Principle
* **Fundamental Postulate of Dimensional Representation:** Every derived physical quantity \\(Q\\) can be uniquely decomposed into a product of powers of the seven SI base quantities:
\\[[Q] = [M]^a [L]^b [T]^c [I]^d [K]^e [\text{mol}]^f [\text{cd}]^g\\]
where \\(a, b, c, d, e, f, g\\) are real numbers representing the dimensions of \\(Q\\) in mass, length, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity respectively [21, 28–30].

---

#### 5. Equation
General dimensional equation:
\\[[Q] = M^a L^b T^c I^d K^e \text{mol}^f \text{cd}^g\\]

Example for Force [20–22]:
\\[\text{Force} = \text{Mass} \times \text{Acceleration} = \text{Mass} \times \frac{\text{Length}}{\text{Time}^2} = M^1 L^1 T^{-2}\\]
\\[[\text{Force}] = [M^1 L^1 T^{-2}]\\]

---

#### 6. Step-by-Step Derivation of Four Major Dimensional Formulae

##### **1. Universal Gravitational Constant (\\(G\\)):**
From Newton's Law of Gravitation: \\(F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \implies G = \frac{F \cdot r^2}{m_1 m_2}\\) [35–36]
\\[[G] = \frac{[F][r]^2}{[m_1][m_2]} = \frac{(M^1 L^1 T^{-2})(L^2)}{M^1 \cdot M^1} = \frac{M^1 L^3 T^{-2}}{M^2} = M^{-1} L^3 T^{-2}\\]

##### **2. Coefficient of Viscosity (\\(\eta\\)):**
From Newton's Law of Viscosity: \\(F = -\eta A \frac{dv}{dx} \implies \eta = \frac{F}{A \left(\frac{dv}{dx}\right)}\\)
\\[[\eta] = \frac{[F]}{[A] \left[\frac{\text{velocity}}{\text{distance}}\right]} = \frac{M^1 L^1 T^{-2}}{L^2 \left(\frac{L T^{-1}}{L}\right)} = \frac{M^1 L^1 T^{-2}}{L^2 T^{-1}} = M^1 L^{-1} T^{-1}\\]

##### **3. Planck's Constant (\\(h\\)):**
From Planck's Quantum Relation: \\(E = h \nu \implies h = \frac{E}{\nu}\\)
\\[[h] = \frac{[\text{Energy}]}{[\text{Frequency}]} = \frac{M^1 L^2 T^{-2}}{T^{-1}} = M^1 L^2 T^{-1}\\]

##### **4. Permittivity of Free Space (\\(\epsilon_0\\)):**
From Coulomb's Law: \\(F = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \implies \epsilon_0 = \frac{q_1 q_2}{4\pi F r^2}\\)
Since charge \\(q = I \cdot t \implies [q] = I^1 T^1\\):
\\[[\epsilon_0] = \frac{(I^1 T^1)^2}{(M^1 L^1 T^{-2})(L^2)} = \frac{I^2 T^2}{M^1 L^3 T^{-2}} = M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2\\]

---

#### 7. Meaning of Variables
* \\(M, L, T, I, K, \text{mol}, \text{cd}\\): Representation symbols for the 7 base physical quantities.
* \\(a, b, c, d, e, f, g\\): Real exponents denoting the dimensions in the respective base quantities.
* \\([ \dots ]\\): Square brackets denoting a dimensional relation.

#### 9. Important H.C. Verma Concepts
* **Magnitude Independence (HCV Sec 1.5):** Dimensional analysis considers only the physical nature of terms, ignoring magnitudes and pure numerical factors.
  * *Example:* Initial velocity \\(u\\), final velocity \\(v\\), average velocity \\(v_{\text{avg}}\\), and change in velocity \\(\Delta v\\) all share the identical dimensional formula \\([L T^{-1}]\\).
* **Unitless vs Dimensionless Rule (HCV Ch 1, Obj I Q4 & Q5):**
  * A **dimensionless quantity MAY have a unit** (e.g., Plane angle has unit radian, Solid angle has unit steradian) [27, 49, 53–54].
  * A **unitless quantity NEVER has dimensions** (a quantity without units is strictly dimensionless, e.g., strain, refractive index).

#### 12. Worked Examples

##### **Worked Example 1.4.1 (Thermodynamics Constant - APPSC Model)**
**Problem:** Find the dimensional formula and SI unit of Stefan's constant (\\(\sigma\\)) from Stefan-Boltzmann Law: \\(E = \sigma A t T^4\\), where \\(E\\) is radiant heat energy, \\(A\\) is surface area, \\(t\\) is time, and \\(T\\) is absolute temperature.

* **Solution:**
  * Rearranging for \\(\sigma\\):
    \\[\sigma = \frac{E}{A \cdot t \cdot T^4}\\]
  * Substituting dimensions:
    \\[[\sigma] = \frac{[E]}{[A][t][T]^4} = \frac{M^1 L^2 T^{-2}}{(L^2)(T^1)(K^4)} = \frac{M^1 L^2 T^{-2}}{L^2 T^1 K^4} = M^1 L^0 T^{-3} K^{-4}\\]
* **SI Unit:** \\(\text{J m}^{-2} \text{s}^{-1} \text{K}^{-4} = \text{W m}^{-2} \text{K}^{-4}\\).
* **Final Answer:** \\([\sigma] = [M^1 L^0 T^{-3} K^{-4}]\\).

##### **Worked Example 1.4.2 (Electromagnetism Constant - HCV Model)**
**Problem (HCV Ch 1 Ex 3c):** Find the dimensional formula of magnetic permeability of free space (\\(\mu_0\\)) using the formula for the magnetic field at the centre of a circular current loop: \\(B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2 a}\\), where \\(B\\) is magnetic field (\\(F = q v B \implies [B] = M^1 L^0 T^{-2} I^{-1}\\)), \\(I\\) is current, and \\(a\\) is radius.

* **Solution:**
  * Rearranging for \\(\mu_0\\):
    \\[\mu_0 = \frac{2 \cdot a \cdot B}{I}\\]
  * Substituting dimensions (2 is a dimensionless number):
    \\[[\mu_0] = \frac{[a][B]}{[I]} = \frac{(L^1)(M^1 L^0 T^{-2} I^{-1})}{I^1} = M^1 L^1 T^{-2} I^{-2}\\]
* **Final Answer:** \\([\mu_0] = [M^1 L^1 T^{-2} I^{-2}]\\).

#### 13. Practice Numericals

##### **Numerical 1 (Easy):**
Find the dimensional formula of (a) Electric Dipole Moment (\\(p = q \cdot d\\)) and (b) Magnetic Dipole Moment (\\(M = I \cdot A\\)).
* **Solution:**
  * (a) \\([p] = [q][d] = (I^1 T^1)(L^1) = M^0 L^1 T^1 I^1\\).
  * (b) \\([M] = [I][A] = (I^1)(L^2) = M^0 L^2 T^0 I^1\\).

##### **Numerical 2 (Moderate - HCV Objective Combination):**
Show dimensionally that the speed of electromagnetic waves in vacuum is given by \\(c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}\\).
* **Solution:**
  * \\([\mu_0] = M^1 L^1 T^{-2} I^{-2}\\)
  * \\([\epsilon_0] = M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2\\)
  * Product \\([\mu_0 \epsilon_0] = (M^1 L^1 T^{-2} I^{-2})(M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2) = M^0 L^{-2} T^2 I^0 = L^{-2} T^2\\)
  * \\([\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}] = \sqrt{L^{-2} T^2} = L^{-1} T^1\\)
  * \\(\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \epsilon_0}}\right] = \frac{1}{L^{-1} T^1} = L^1 T^{-1}\\)
  * Since \\([L T^{-1}]\\) is the dimensional formula of velocity, the relation is dimensionally verified.

##### **Numerical 3 (APPSC Level - Fine Structure Constant):**
Find the dimensional formula of the dimensionless constant known as the Fine Structure Constant: \\(\alpha = \frac{e^2}{2 \epsilon_0 h c}\\), where \\(e\\) is elementary charge, \\(\epsilon_0\\) is permittivity of free space, \\(h\\) is Planck's constant, and \\(c\\) is speed of light.
* **Solution:**
  * \\([e^2] = I^2 T^2\\)
  * \\([\epsilon_0] = M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2\\)
  * \\([h] = M^1 L^2 T^{-1}\\)
  * \\([c] = L^1 T^{-1}\\)
  * Denominator \\([\epsilon_0 h c] = (M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2)(M^1 L^2 T^{-1})(L^1 T^{-1}) = M^0 L^0 T^2 I^2 = I^2 T^2\\)
  * Ratio \\([\alpha] = \frac{I^2 T^2}{I^2 T^2} = M^0 L^0 T^0\\) (Pure dimensionless number \\(\alpha \approx \frac{1}{137}\\)).

#### 14. APPSC Level MCQs

##### **MCQ 1 (Identical Pair Classification)**
Which of the following pairs of physical quantities does **NOT** have identical dimensional formulas?
(A) Work and Torque  
(B) Impulse and Linear Momentum  
(C) Stress and Young's Modulus  
(D) Angular Momentum and Latent Heat  

* **Answer:** **(D)**
* **Explanation:** Angular momentum has dimensions \\([M^1 L^2 T^{-1}]\\), whereas Latent Heat has dimensions \\([M^0 L^2 T^{-2}]\\). Options A (\\(M^1 L^2 T^{-2}\\)), B (\\(M^1 L^1 T^{-1}\\)), and C (\\(M^1 L^{-1} T^{-2}\\)) all share identical dimensional formulas within their respective pairs [187–188].

##### **MCQ 2 (Conceptual Rule - HCV Objective I Q4 & Q5)**
Consider the following statements:
1. A physical quantity can be unitless but possess non-zero dimensions.
2. A physical quantity can be dimensionless but possess a non-zero unit.
3. Torque and Work have the same dimensions, but represent different physical concepts.

Which of the statements given above are **CORRECT**?
(A) 1 and 2 only  
(B) 2 and 3 only  
(C) 1 and 3 only  
(D) 1, 2, and 3  

* **Answer:** **(B)**
* **Explanation:** Statement 1 is false because a unitless quantity can *never* have non-zero dimensions. Statement 2 is true (e.g., plane angle has unit radian but is dimensionless). Statement 3 is true (both are \\(M^1 L^2 T^{-2}\\), but work is a scalar energy while torque is a vector turning moment) [47, 187–188].

##### **MCQ 3 (Electrodynamics Constants)**
The dimensional formula \\([M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2]\\) corresponds to which of the following physical constants?
(A) Permeability of free space (\\(\mu_0\\))  
(B) Universal Gravitational Constant (\\(G\\))  
(C) Permittivity of free space (\\(\epsilon_0\\))  
(D) Electrical Conductivity (\\(\sigma\\))  

* **Answer:** **(C)**
* **Explanation:** From Coulomb's Law, \\(\epsilon_0 = \frac{q^2}{4\pi F r^2} = \frac{I^2 T^2}{(M^1 L^1 T^{-2})(L^2)} = M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2\\).
