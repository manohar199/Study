### EVALUATION & SOLUTION OF ACTIVE RECALL QUESTION 1.2

1. **Electric Current vs. Electric Charge as Base Quantity:**
   Although electric charge is a more fundamental property of matter, **Electric Current (Ampere)** was chosen as the SI base quantity because current can be measured experimentally with far higher precision, stability, and reproducibility (using magnetic force per unit length or electro-dynamometers) than static electric charge.

2. **Derivation of Mass and Length in terms of \\((F, a, T)\\):**
   * **Mass \\(([M])\\):** From Newton's Second Law, \\(F = m \cdot a \implies M = \frac{F}{a} = [F^1 a^{-1} T^0]\\).
   * **Length \\(([L])\\):** From kinematic relation, \\(a = \frac{L}{T^2} \implies L = a \cdot T^2 = [F^0 a^1 T^2]\\).

3. **Validity of \\((Work, Force, Displacement)\\) as Base Set:**
   **No**, the set \\((\text{Work}, \text{Force}, \text{Displacement})\\) **cannot** form a valid fundamental set because \\(\text{Work} = \text{Force} \times \text{Displacement}\\). Since Work is directly derived from Force and Displacement, these three quantities are not mutually independent.

### SUBTOPIC 1.3: SI UNITS, MULTIPLES AND SUB-MULTIPLES, RULES FOR WRITING S.I. UNITS, ADVANTAGES OF SI UNITS

---

#### 1. Concept
Before the establishment of a single worldwide standard, different countries used different systems of units, such as the **FPS** (Foot-Pound-Second), **CGS** (Centimetre-Gram-Second), and **MKS** (Metre-Kilogram-Second) systems [26–27]. This diversity created confusion in international trade, technology transfer, and scientific communication.

To resolve these barriers, the **Conférence Générale des Poids et Mesures (CGPM)** (General Conference on Weights and Measures) adopted the **Le Système International d'Unités (SI)** in 1971 [26–27]. The SI system provides a standardized, logical, and universally accepted system of units based on 7 base units, 2 supplementary units, standardized powers-of-ten prefixes, and strict typographic rules [26–28].

---

#### 2. Definition
* **SI System (Système International d'Unités):** An international, modernized metric system built on seven defined base units, two supplementary units, and derived units formed by coherent combinations without numerical factors [26–27].
* **SI Prefixes (Multiples and Sub-multiples):** Standardized prefixes representing powers of \\(10\\) (ranging from \\(10^{-24}\\) to \\(10^{24}\\)) attached to the name of a unit to express extremely large or small magnitudes conveniently.
* **Rules for Writing SI Units:** A set of international typographical conventions mandated by the CGPM to eliminate ambiguity in scientific literature and technical documents.
* **Advantages of SI:** The key attributes that make SI superior to older systems: it is **Rational**, **Coherent**, **Metric**, and **Universal**.

---

#### 3. Physical Meaning
The physical universe operates across vast spatial and temporal scales:
* The diameter of a proton is \\(\approx 10^{-15}\text{ m}\\) (femtometre).
* The distance to a distant galaxy is \\(\approx 10^{26}\text{ m}\\).
* The mass of an electron is \\(\approx 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}\\).

Writing such values with long strings of zeroes is cumbersome and error-prone. Standard **SI Prefixes** allow scientists to scale units easily (e.g., \\(20\ \mu\text{F}\\) instead of \\(0.000020\text{ F}\\)). Furthermore, strict **typographical rules** prevent commercial and technical misunderstandings across different languages.

---

#### 4. Law / Principle

##### **A. Principle of Coherence:**
A system of units is **coherent** if all derived units are formed by simple multiplication and division of base units with a numerical factor of exactly **\\(1\\)**.
\\[\text{Unit of Derived Quantity} = (1\text{ Base Unit}_1)^a \times (1\text{ Base Unit}_2)^b \times \dots\\]

##### **B. Principle of Rationalization:**
A system of units is **rationalized** if it assigns **one and only one unit to one physical quantity**, regardless of its form. In SI, **Joule (J)** is the sole unit for all forms of energy (mechanical, thermal, electrical, chemical, nuclear). In older systems, mechanical energy was measured in *Ergs*, thermal energy in *Calories*, and electrical energy in *Kilowatt-hours*.

---

#### 5. Equation

* **Prefix Relation:**
\\[\text{Magnitude} = n \times (10^k \cdot u)\\]
where \\(10^k\\) is the multiplier corresponding to a specific SI prefix.

* **Coherent Derived Unit Equation:**
\\[1\text{ Joule} = 1\text{ kg} \cdot \text{m}^2 \cdot \text{s}^{-2}\\]
\\[1\text{ Watt} = 1\text{ J} \cdot \text{s}^{-1} = 1\text{ kg} \cdot \text{m}^2 \cdot \text{s}^{-3}\\]

---

#### 6. Derivation: Converting Derived Units in Rationalized vs. Non-Rationalized Systems

In the older CGS/Caloric system, heat energy \\(Q\\) and mechanical work \\(W\\) required a conversion factor known as the **Mechanical Equivalent of Heat (\\(J\\))**:
\\[W = J \cdot Q\\]
where \\(J \approx 4.186\text{ Joules/calorie}\\).

In the modern **rationalized SI system**, heat is recognized directly as energy in transit. Therefore, \\(J = 1\\) (dimensionless constant of unity):
\\[W = Q \quad (\text{both measured strictly in Joules})\\]

---

#### 7. Meaning of Variables
* \\(10^k\\): Power of ten scaling factor.
* \\(n\\): Numerical magnitude.
* \\(u\\): Fundamental or derived base SI unit.

#### 9. Official Rules for Writing S.I. Units (APPSC Direct Exam Authority)

1. **Full Names of Units:** Full names of units are always written in lower case, even if named after a scientist.
   * *Correct:* newton, joule, watt, pascal, ampere, kelvin.
   * *Incorrect:* Newton, Joule, Watt, Pascal, Ampere, Kelvin.
2. **Symbols Named After Scientists:** Symbols for units named after a scientist are capitalized; all other unit symbols are written in lower case.
   * *Correct:* \\(\text{N}\\) (newton), \\(\text{J}\\) (joule), \\(\text{W}\\) (watt), \\(\text{Pa}\\) (pascal), \\(\text{A}\\) (ampere), \\(\text{K}\\) (kelvin), \\(\text{m}\\) (metre), \\(\text{s}\\) (second), \\(\text{cd}\\) (candela).
   * *Incorrect:* \\(\text{n}\\) for newton, \\(\text{j}\\) for joule, \\(\text{M}\\) for metre, \\(\text{S}\\) for second.
3. **No Plural Suffix in Symbols:** Symbols are never pluralized with an 's'.
   * *Correct:* \\(50\text{ kg}\\), \\(10\text{ m}\\), \\(100\text{ N}\\).
   * *Incorrect:* \\(50\text{ kgs}\\), \\(10\text{ ms}\\) (this means 10 milliseconds!), \\(100\text{ Ns}\\).
4. **No Full Stops / Punctuation in Symbols:** Symbols do not take full stops or periods at the end unless at the end of a sentence.
   * *Correct:* \\(5\text{ kg}\\), \\(12\text{ cm}\\).
   * *Incorrect:* \\(5\text{ kg.}\\), \\(12\text{ cm.}\\).
5. **Solidus (Slash) Usage:** Not more than one solidus (\\(/\\)) should be used in a derived unit expression to avoid ambiguity.
   * *Correct:* \\(\text{J} \cdot \text{K}^{-1} \cdot \text{mol}^{-1}\\) or \\(\text{J}/(\text{K} \cdot \text{mol})\\).
   * *Incorrect:* \\(\text{J}/\text{K}/\text{mol}\\).
6. **Space Between Number and Unit:** A space must be left between the numerical magnitude and the unit symbol.
   * *Correct:* \\(10\text{ MW}\\), \\(25\text{ kg}\\), \\(300\text{ K}\\).
   * *Incorrect:* \\(10\text{MW}\\), \\(25\text{kg}\\).
7. **No Compound Prefixes:** Double or compound prefixes are strictly prohibited.
   * *Correct:* \\(1\text{ pF}\\) (picofarad), \\(1\text{ nm}\\) (nanometre).
   * *Incorrect:* \\(1\ \mu\mu\text{F}\\) (micromicrofarad), \\(1\text{ m}\mu\text{m}\\) (millimicron).
8. **Prefix Belongs to the Unit:** When a prefix is attached to a unit with an exponent, the exponent applies to both the prefix and the unit.
   * *Example:* \\(1\text{ cm}^3 = (10^{-2}\text{ m})^3 = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^3\\). (Not \\(10^{-2}\text{ m}^3\\)).

#### 10. Advantages of SI Units

1. **Coherent System:** All derived units are obtained by straightforward multiplication or division of base units without introducing awkward conversion numbers [21–22].
2. **Rationalized System:** Assigns a single unified unit to each physical quantity (e.g., Joule for all forms of energy).
3. **Metric System:** Decimal-based system; multiples and sub-multiples are expressible in powers of 10.
4. **Comprehensive / Universal:** Covers all branches of science and engineering (mechanics, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, optics, atomic physics) [26–27].
5. **Absolute System:** Does not depend on gravity (\\(g\\)), unlike gravitational systems (e.g., kg-force, pound-force).

#### 16. Worked Examples

##### **Worked Example 1.3.1 (SI Writing Rules - APPSC Standard)**
**Problem:** Identify which of the following expressions violates official SI rules, and state the rule violated:
1. \\(100\text{ Ns}\\) for force
2. \\(\text{Acceleration} = 9.8\text{ m/s/s}\\)
3. \\(\text{Capacitance} = 5\ \mu\mu\text{F}\\)
4. \\(\text{Energy} = 50\text{ Joules}\\)

* **Solution & Rule Analysis:**
  1. \\(100\text{ Ns}\\) violates **Rule 3** (Pluralization). Symbols cannot be pluralized. (Note: \\(\text{N s}\\) with a space means Newton-seconds, but \\(\text{Ns}\\) as plural for Newtons is wrong).
  2. \\(9.8\text{ m/s/s}\\) violates **Rule 5** (Solidus Rule). More than one solidus is not permitted. It must be written as \\(\text{m/s}^2\\) or \\(\text{m} \cdot \text{s}^{-2}\\).
  3. \\(5\ \mu\mu\text{F}\\) violates **Rule 7** (Compound Prefixes). Double prefixes are forbidden. It must be written as \\(5\text{ pF}\\).
  4. \\(50\text{ Joules}\\) violates **Rule 1** (Full Name Capitalization). Full names must be in lower case (\\(50\text{ joules}\\)).

##### **Worked Example 1.3.2 (Prefix Volume Conversion)**
**Problem:** A container has a volume of \\(5\text{ cm}^3\\). Express this volume in SI units (\\(\text{m}^3\\)) using prefix exponential expansion.

* **Solution:**
  * \\(1\text{ cm} = 10^{-2}\text{ m}\\)
  * \\(1\text{ cm}^3 = (10^{-2}\text{ m})^3 = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^3\\)
  * Volume \\(V = 5 \times (1\text{ cm}^3) = 5 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^3\\).
* **Final Answer:** \\(5 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^3\\).

#### 17. Practice Numericals

##### **Numerical 1 (Easy):**
Convert a frequency of \\(100\text{ MHz}\\) and a capacitance of \\(25\text{ pF}\\) into their basic SI unit values without prefixes.
* **Solution:**
  * Frequency \\(f = 100\text{ MHz} = 100 \times 10^6\text{ Hz} = 10^8\text{ Hz}\\).
  * Capacitance \\(C = 25\text{ pF} = 25 \times 10^{-12}\text{ F}\\).

##### **Numerical 2 (Moderate):**
An industrial plant in Visakhapatnam discharges water at a rate of \\(72\text{ m}^3/\text{hour}\\). Convert this flow rate into \\(\text{cm}^3/\text{s}\\) and \\(\text{L/s}\\).
* **Solution:**
  * \\(1\text{ m}^3 = (10^2\text{ cm})^3 = 10^6\text{ cm}^3\\).
  * \\(1\text{ hour} = 3600\text{ s}\\).
  * Flow rate \\(Q = \frac{72 \times 10^6\text{ cm}^3}{3600\text{ s}} = 20,000\text{ cm}^3/\text{s} = 2 \times 10^4\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}\\).
  * Since \\(1\text{ Litre} = 1000\text{ cm}^3 = 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3\\):
    \\[\text{Flow rate} = \frac{20,000\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}}{1000\text{ cm}^3/\text{L}} = 20\text{ L/s}\\]

##### **Numerical 3 (APPSC Level):**
A radio station transmits at a wavelength of \\(\lambda = 300\text{ m}\\). Calculate the frequency of the wave in \\(\text{kHz}\\) and \\(\text{MHz}\\), taking the speed of light \\(c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}\\).
* **Solution:**
  * Wave equation: \\(c = f \cdot \lambda \implies f = \frac{c}{\lambda}\\).
  * \\(f = \frac{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}}{300\text{ m}} = 10^6\text{ Hz}\\).
  * In \\(\text{kHz}\\): \\(f = \frac{10^6}{10^3} = 1000\text{ kHz}\\).
  * In \\(\text{MHz}\\): \\(f = \frac{10^6}{10^6} = 1\text{ MHz}\\).

##### **MCQ 3 (Conceptual Statement)**
Consider the following statements regarding SI advantages:

2. SI is a coherent system because derived units are obtained without arbitrary numerical multipliers.
